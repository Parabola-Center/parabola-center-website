Student-First AI Policy

Students are not test subjects. Our model framework gives lawmakers, school boards, and families a clear way to make sure AI in schools serves students, not the technology companies selling it.

Read the principles Endorse the policy

Why student-first

Schools are being sold AI faster than anyone can prove it’s safe

Generative AI products are arriving in classrooms through vendor contracts, free pilots, and features quietly switched on inside tools schools already use. Students rarely get a say, families are rarely asked, and there is little independent evidence that these products improve learning.

The Student-First AI Policy flips the default. Instead of asking whether a product can be stopped, it asks companies to show their product is safe, effective, and respectful of children’s privacy before it ever reaches a student.

AI tools in schools are products, and the companies that make them should be accountable for how they affect children.

The framework

Six principles for AI in schools

  1. Evidence before adoption

    Student-facing AI products should not be deployed until independent research shows they are safe and actually improve learning.

  2. Student data stays protected

    No training AI models on students’ data, no targeted advertising, and strict limits on how long student information is kept.

  3. No manipulative design

    No companion-style chatbots, emotional dependence features, or engagement tricks designed to keep children using a product.

  4. Clear disclosure

    Students and families always know when they are interacting with AI, and schools publish a list of every AI tool in use.

  5. Teachers and families decide

    Educators keep professional judgment over teaching and grading, and families can opt their children out without penalty.

  6. Vendors are accountable

    Companies carry responsibility when their products harm students, with independent audits and real enforcement behind the rules.

Who it’s for

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Does this policy ban AI in schools?

No. It sets the conditions AI products must meet before they reach students: proven benefit, strong privacy protections, honest design, and real accountability. Teachers can still teach about AI.

Why focus on students rather than technology?

Children can’t meaningfully consent to experimental products, and schools often adopt tools faster than they can evaluate them. Putting students first means the burden of proof sits with the companies, not with kids.

How does this connect to the call to pause GenAI in schools?

Parabola Center signed the open statement calling for a five-year pause on student-facing generative AI. The Student-First AI Policy describes the standards products should meet before that pause is lifted.

Who is Parabola Center?

Parabola Center for Law and Policy is a nonprofit of legal, public health, and policy professionals working to put people over profits in emerging industries, from marijuana to artificial intelligence.
